Get to know the language

What is Cherokee?

Cherokee, also called Tsalagi, is an Indigenous language of the Cherokee people. It has its own writing system, called the Cherokee syllabary.

⌖

Where it is spoken

Today, speakers and learners are mainly centred in northeastern Oklahoma and western North Carolina.

Ꭰ

A unique writing system

Cherokee uses its own syllabary, a writing system in which each character represents a syllable.

♡

A living culture

Language carries stories, knowledge, identity and ways of expressing ideas across generations.

Why action matters

Every generation matters.

Our class research used an estimate of around 2,000 first-language speakers. Estimates can change, and speaker counts depend on how fluency is measured. The important point is that passing the language to younger generations matters.

The challenge

Why is Cherokee at risk?

Language loss has many causes. Historical pressure and changes in everyday language use have both affected Cherokee.

Forced removal

The removal of Cherokee people from their homelands disrupted communities, families and cultural practices.

Boarding schools

Many Indigenous children were pressured or punished for speaking their languages and were pushed to use English.

Less daily use

English is widely used in education, entertainment and online spaces. When a language is used less at home and in public, fewer young people may learn it.

Globalization can also help. Websites, digital lessons and social media can connect learners across countries with Cherokee-led language resources.
